What Shariah screening costs (and buys) on the ASX

S&P/ASX 200 Shariah vs the S&P/ASX 200, by the numbers

Data as of 30 June 2026 · S&P Dow Jones Indices Shariah Scorecard

The S&P/ASX 200 Shariah index applies AAOIFI-based screens to the S&P/ASX 200: it drops companies in prohibited sectors (conventional banking and insurance, alcohol, gambling, and similar) and then filters the rest on debt, cash/interest-bearing securities, and receivables, each capped near a third of market cap. What's left is an 80-stock, real-money-tracked version of the ASX 200 — not a backtest, an actual published index. Comparing the two shows what that screen costs in return, and where.

Valuation & composition

The Shariah index is cheaper on every multiple but one, and pays a lower dividend — a side effect of excluding banks, which are ASX 200 heavyweights and typically high-yield.

Reading it straight

Over three and five years, the Shariah-screened version of the ASX 200 has both returned less and moved around more than the index it's screened from. The likely mechanic is composition, not the screen being "worse" at picking stocks: the exclusions cut out conventional banks and insurers almost entirely, and Australian banks had a strong multi-year run over this exact window while making up roughly a quarter of the ASX 200's weight. A basket that can't hold that sector was always going to feel it when that sector leads. QTD and YTD tell a different, more recent story — Shariah ahead by 3.0 and 0.5 points — so the gap isn't fixed in one direction, it moves with which sectors are leading.

None of this is a verdict on whether Shariah-compliant investing is "worth it." That's a values decision, not a returns one. What the data supports is narrower and more useful: over this specific window, compliance had a measurable, quantifiable cost on the ASX, concentrated in the sector it excludes.

Methodology & limitations

Figures are drawn directly from S&P Dow Jones Indices' quarterly Shariah scorecard (report date 30 June 2026), which tracks the official S&P/ASX 200 Shariah index against its S&P/ASX 200 base index. Screening follows the S&P Shariah Indices methodology: prohibited-sector exclusion (conventional banking/finance, alcohol, gambling, adult entertainment, pork, tobacco, weapons) followed by three financial ratio caps — debt, cash and interest-bearing securities, and accounts receivable, each roughly capped near 33% (49% for receivables) of market capitalisation.

  • This is published index-level data, not a stock-by-stock backtest built here — that's a deliberate choice: an official, audited index is a stronger source than a DIY basket over unverified historical prices.
  • Sharpe ratios use a single current risk-free rate (RBA cash rate, 4.35% as of the most recent decision before this report) applied flatly across 3- and 5-year windows, rather than the actual prevailing rate in each period.
  • Returns and volatility are in AUD, total return, as published; no fees, transaction costs, or tracking error versus any specific fund are reflected.
  • Past performance over this window is not a forecast — the QTD/YTD reversal shows the gap is regime-dependent, not structural.
